# D36 — A secondary is asked for its serial after it is notified, not on a sweep

- Decided: 2026-09-02

## Context

D34 writes the configuration a secondary needs and hands it over, and says the obvious next
question is not its business: "A serial probe against the configured targets, and the lag
metric it would feed, is a separate feature with its own questions about timeouts and about
who does the asking."

The fault it would catch is the one the generated configuration cannot rule out. Everything
this server can check about a transfer it checks on its own side: the key exists, the address
is allowed, the zone is served. Whether the secondary took the copy is a fact that lives on
the other machine, and a secondary that answers NOTIFY and then fails to transfer looks
exactly like one that is working.

## Decision

**A probe is a SOA query, unsigned, over UDP, to the address in the notify list.** TSIG signs
what this server sends to a secondary that has a key, because a NOTIFY it acts on is
forgeable. A SOA query is not that: nothing is being asked for beyond what the secondary
answers anyone, and BIND and Knot do not expect our key on a query. The configuration
generated by D34 adds no query restriction, so an installation that has one globally answers
nothing, and that reads the same as unreachable, which is honest.

**A pair of target and zone becomes due when its notification finishes**, answered or
abandoned, which the notifier already tracks per zone. A zone nobody edits produces no
probes, and the traffic is proportional to how much this server is changed rather than to how
many zones it holds. While a target is behind on a zone the probe repeats on a backoff, and
stops when the serials agree.

**One query, a short fixed wait, and the backoff is the retry.** D34 named timeouts as one
of the two questions this feature carries. A probe that retransmits inside itself would be a
second retry schedule beside the backoff above, tuned by nobody and disagreeing with it under
load. The wait is a few seconds rather than one, because D34's secondaries are other people's
machines and some of them are across a link, and a no-answer is not a fault declaration on
its own: the pair is asked again, and one that is genuinely unreachable says so by saying
nothing repeatedly.

**A slow sweep is the floor**, so that no pair goes unasked for longer than an hour. This
catches the secondary that quietly lost a zone it already had, where nothing changed here and
so nothing would ever become due. Spread across the hour it costs one query per zone per
target, and the notify list is filled by hand (D27), so the multiplier is a handful rather
than a population.

**Nothing is reported while a notification is outstanding.** A zone changed a moment ago
whose secondary has not transferred yet is not behind, it is in flight, and a metric that
cannot tell the two apart is one an operator learns to ignore.

**In a cluster, the leader probes and the other nodes do not.** The notify list replicates
(D32), so every node would otherwise ask the same question and publish its own answer to
one fact. This is settled here rather than left to the clustering work, because an open
question in a record is one that gets reopened at the worst moment.

**Nothing automatic follows from a lagging secondary.** Not another NOTIFY: a secondary that
is not transferring does not transfer because it was told twice.

## Consequences

Two metrics rather than one. `weg_secondary_serial_lag` is a gauge, labelled by target and
zone, and it is set only where an answer came back, because a gauge has no value that means
"did not answer". The outcome of a probe is counted separately, the way `ObserveNotify`
already counts the steps of telling a secondary. Cardinality is targets times zones, bounded
by a notify list that starts empty and is filled by hand (D27).

A gauge outlives what it describes. A target dropped from the notify list, or a zone deleted,
has to have its series removed rather than left at the last value it had, which is the kind
of thing that is noticed a year later when somebody trusts the number.

`weg secondary` gains somewhere to show this. It is the noun for the other end of a transfer
already, and until now everything under it has been about writing configuration rather than
about what the other end is doing.

The probe sends DNS messages and belongs beside the notifier in `internal/dns`, which is
already the one part of that package that sends rather than answers. It is control plane:
nothing here runs on the path of a query (invariant 2).

## Where this stands

The prober is built, and the metrics are not shaped the way the consequences above assumed.
There is no zone label. `ObserveNotify` had already settled that question in the same file
and the other way round, because the zone count is what makes a label expensive, and a
reason that holds for counting notifications holds here. What is exported is per secondary:
`weg_secondary_serial_lag` is the furthest behind of its zones, `weg_secondary_zones_behind`
how many they are, and the probe outcomes are counted beside them. Which zone it is, this
record still owes, and it is owed to the API and the two clients rather than to a scrape.

There are three gauges rather than the two above. A secondary that has gone quiet reports
nothing behind, because nothing about it is known, and that is indistinguishable from health
unless something says so: `weg_secondary_zones_unanswered` is what says so.
