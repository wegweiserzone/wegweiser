# Code of Conduct

This is a one-person project. There is no committee behind this document and no process to
appeal to: there is me, reading what arrives and deciding what to do about it. Saying so up
front seems better than implying an apparatus that does not exist.

## What is expected

Assume the person on the other end is trying to do something reasonable. Most disagreements
here are about DNS, and DNS is full of behaviour that looks wrong until you find the RFC
paragraph it comes from. Ask what somebody meant before deciding they are careless.

Criticism of code is welcome and is the point of a review. Criticism of a person is not the
same thing, and the difference is usually visible in whether the sentence would still make
sense with the code removed from it.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, whether it is in an issue, a pull request, a commit message or a private message
that came out of one of them. Sexual attention nobody asked for. Insults about who somebody
is rather than about what they wrote. Publishing somebody's private information. Persisting
after being asked to stop.

None of that becomes acceptable for being technically correct, funny, or a reply to someone
who started it.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project is: this repository, its issues and pull requests, its security
advisories, and anywhere somebody is representing Wegweiser. It does not extend to what
people do elsewhere, with one exception: conduct outside the project that makes somebody
unsafe inside it is the project's business.

## Reporting

conduct@wegweiser.zone

Tell me what happened and where. A link is worth more than a summary, because it survives an
edit. If you would rather the person did not learn who reported it, say so and I will work
around it; if that turns out to be impossible I will tell you before doing anything, not
after.

I usually answer within a few days. Nothing is passed on to anybody else, because there is
nobody else.

A report about me has nowhere good to go, which is the honest weakness of a project this
size. GitHub takes reports about conduct on the platform itself, and that route does not run
through me.

## What happens then

What is warranted, which depends on what happened: a word in private, an edited or deleted
comment, or a block. I will say which and why to the person concerned. Repeating something
after being asked to stop is what turns a word into a block.

There is no formal appeal. If you think I got it wrong, say so and I will look again.
